# Real-data PyTorch adaptation, 2026-09-15

This continues [the untouched-model implementation report](implementation-2026-09-15.md)
and [PR 94](https://github.com/FluidInference/mobius/pull/94). The user explicitly
changed the immediate deliverable to **a trained PyTorch model with the correct
weights**. Core ML, Swift source transfer, and Apple hardware are outside this
run's acceptance criteria. The original demo remains unchanged.

## What actually ran

The designated RTX A6000 trained the real Kokoro v1.1-zh generator. No synthetic
recordings were substituted for training audio. The released base checkpoint,
configuration, vocabulary, and voice were first verified against the pinned
hashes, with exact independent upstream inference parity on 15 cases.

The implementation now includes real recording acquisition/preprocessing,
supervised forward/loss, gradient checks, small-set overfit, a resumable optimizer,
bounded full-data adaptation, checkpoint export, checksum-verified inference,
and baseline/candidate/reference speech evaluation. Heavy weights, real recordings,
private manifests, and raw audio stay in ignored local artifact directories.

## Data decision and provenance

speaker, with the largest single-microphone volume among the female speakers
the corpus authors selected for their lower-foreign-accent experiments. It is
an adaptation experiment; it is not a new claim that MF5 is an approved production
voice. See the [original corpus](https://www.emime.org/participate/emime-bilingual-database.html),

- Archive bytes: 1,384,527,144. Only MF5 microphone-0 originals and supporting
  metadata were extracted; microphone-1 copies and segmented test copies do

## Versioned frontend and acoustic targets

The PyTorch frontend uses pinned Misaki 0.9.4, Kokoro's Mandarin 1.1 token
convention, the installed English spaCy model, and eSpeak fallback. It applies
NFKC and traditional-to-simplified Chinese conversion before phonemization.
`API` is explicitly letter-spelled; `GitHub` has an explicit pronunciation.
Numeral 二 remains independent of true 儿 suffix handling. English-only numerals
use the English frontend. Unknown symbols, empty text, and overlength input
fail; no tokens are silently dropped or truncated. The model vocabulary is
unchanged. This is a Python frontend contract, not a claim of Swift parity.

Real audio is rationally resampled with pinned SciPy `resample_poly(160,147)`
to 24 kHz and right-padded by fewer than 600 samples. Target files preserve
the real waveform, IDs, durations, F0, and energy with SHA-256 evidence.

Alignment uses the untouched model's temporary generated waveform and its
token durations, then monotone MFCC DTW against the actual recording. Only
the **real waveform** is used as a reconstruction target. The generated
waveform is alignment scaffolding, not an acquired or training recording.
Durations are positive integers, capped at 50 per token, and sum exactly to
the real waveform's 600-sample frame grid. This is approximate alignment,
not an independently annotated phoneme aligner.

F0 uses the pinned StyleTTS2 JDC checkpoint. Mel preprocessing deliberately
matches StyleTTS2's published 80-bin, 2,048-FFT, 1,200-window, 300-hop convention,
including the default 16 kHz mel-filter parameter applied to native 24 kHz
audio. Energy follows its `log(norm(exp(normalized_mel * 4 - 4)))` target.
Pitch and energy have twice the duration-grid resolution. See the
[pinned upstream training reference](https://github.com/yl4579/StyleTTS2/blob/5cedc71c333f8d8b8551ca59378bdcc7af4c9529/train_finetune.py).

Eight real-recording integration cases (four per language) checked every
trainable gradient, absence of gradients on frozen parameters, exact supervised
versus free-running forward parity using the same original durations/RNG, and
DTW controls. All eight correct transcript/audio pairs had lower DTW cost than
wrong-text speech from the same speaker/language. Self-alignment recovered the
original token durations exactly. This calibrates basic discrimination only;
it does not establish phoneme boundary accuracy.

## Optimization

The supervised path bypasses rounded integer duration prediction with the
real-aligned target durations. Gradients reach the ALBERT projection, prosody
predictor, and text encoder through the frozen decoder. ALBERT and the decoder
remain frozen; all 140 export-workaround identity affine constants remain
frozen. A single shared 256-dimensional residual adapts the original voice
table; it does not add a multi-speaker inference interface.

Loss: five times normalized log-mel L1, duration survival BCE, smooth-L1
log-duration, voiced F0 smooth-L1, 0.1 times unvoiced F0, energy smooth-L1,
and 0.01 times voice-residual L2. The 50 Hz voiced threshold is an operational
proxy, not a validated Mandarin tone classifier. Training uses FP32, real
2.4-second crops, finite loss/gradient checks, gradient clipping at 5, and AdamW.
The sampling RNG balances languages before choosing a real utterance.

| Run | Real training utterances | Updates | LR | Voice residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Micro-overfit | 8, four per language | 100 | 1e-4 | fixed zero |
| Pilot v1, exploratory | 225 | 1,000 completed | 2e-5 | LR 1e-3 |
| Pilot v2, deterministic delivery candidates | 225 | 500 completed | 2e-5 | LR 1e-3 |

Both use accumulation of two utterances per update. The micro run's fixed
evaluation loss decreased **6.79435 → 3.86790 (43.1%)**; English decreased
8.12326 → 3.98156 and Mandarin 5.46544 → 3.75424. Frozen-parameter hashes stayed
identical and trainable-parameter hashes changed. Peak allocation was 1.60 GB.
The micro checkpoint is a harness test, not the selected deliverable.

Full-data snapshots and validation occur every 100 updates. Resume checkpoints
include model, optimizer, exact CPU/CUDA RNG states, sampling RNG, update number,
best validation score, and the manifest/config contract. Resume rejects changed
data or optimization settings. Checkpoints are atomically replaced.

### Reproducibility failure and correction

The first resume test failed even though CPU/CUDA/sampling RNG states matched.
Strict deterministic execution exposed CUDA `reflection_pad1d_backward` in
both the mel-loss STFT and upstream decoder. Their atomic gradient accumulation
produced small update differences between processes. These were not hidden by
looser tolerances.

The training wrapper now replaces the decoder's parameter-free `(1,0)` reflection
padding with exact slice/concatenation. Mel analysis uses the equivalent explicit
reflected slices before `center=False` STFT. Unit checks establish exact forward
values and CPU gradients; the real-recording integration checks still pass.
Strict deterministic algorithms remain enabled rather than warning-only.

The corrected restart proof compares three uninterrupted real optimizer updates
with one update followed by a fresh process and two resumed updates. **Every
model tensor, optimizer tensor/state, CPU/CUDA RNG state, sampling RNG state,
step, and best validation value matches bit-for-bit.** A three-recording
preprocessing replay also reproduces every saved audio/ID/duration/F0/energy
tensor exactly. This proves the tested restart boundary/environment, not
cross-hardware numerical identity.

Pilot v1 is retained as exploratory evidence. Pilot v2 starts afresh from the
base checkpoint using the corrected deterministic path. The
[selection policy](../evaluation-policy.json) was written before evaluating its
candidates: compare updates 100/300/500 by mean development English WER and
Mandarin CER, break ties with acoustic validation loss, reject invalid signals,
and report all controls. Test passages do not influence selection. Diagnostic
and duplicate checkpoint retention is bounded; their hashes and cleanup record
remain in local run evidence.

## Evaluation interpretation

The free-running evaluator synthesizes the same text, frontend, style-row rule,
seed, speed, and FP32 generator tensors for baseline and candidate. Evaluation
disables matmul TF32 and retains the pinned PyTorch cuDNN convolution default
(TF32 allowed); training disables both. ASR uses FP16 on this GPU. Real source recordings
are transcribed as a reference ASR floor. The pinned evaluator is
[Whisper large-v3-turbo](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo),
revision `41f01f3fe87f28c78e2fbf8b568835947dd65ed9`, greedy decoding, explicit
English or Chinese transcription, no sampling, and no audio truncation.
All ASR weights/config/tokenizer files are checksummed before execution.

Reported English WER and Mandarin/mixed CER use NFKC, simplified Chinese,
lowercase, and punctuation removal. Numeral spellings are not equated. Twelve
project-authored controls cover API, GitHub, numerals, 儿, and six mixed utterances.
They are development diagnostics, not an independently held-out benchmark.

The forced-Chinese decoder sometimes translates an English-heavy mixed utterance;
forcing English preserves its English words but romanizes the Mandarin phrase.
The raw diagnostics retain this behavior. A language-sensitivity probe is
recorded separately; it is not used to silently replace unfavorable transcripts.
ASR scores therefore do not certify code-switch naturalness, correct Mandarin
tones, speaker identity, or perceived audio quality.

## Remaining production boundary

A loadable trained checkpoint and passing implementation checks are distinct
from production qualification. This corpus does not provide independently
confirmed production voice consent, enough real code-switch coverage,
session-disjoint testing, or bilingual listening/tone acceptance. These limits
are carried into the bundle manifest. No Apple/ONNX conversion or deployment
is implied by this PyTorch delivery.

## Frozen-model held-out result

The selected model hash was fixed before evaluating all 74 official/grouped test
utterances. It was not reselected after seeing these results.

| Test metric | Untouched baseline | Trained model | Real-recording ASR reference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| English WER, 43 utterances / 325 words | 6.15% (20 errors) | **4.31% (14)** | 5.23% (17) |
| Mandarin CER, 31 utterances / 466 characters | **1.50% (7 errors)** | 1.93% (9) | 1.07% (5) |

English improves in this ASR proxy; Mandarin has two additional character
errors. The small single-session corpus and decoder limitations do not support
a broad quality-superiority claim. The checkpoint is delivered as a verified,
actually trained PyTorch adaptation, **not production-qualified speech**.
See [complete held-out counts](../evidence/heldout-test.json). A real recording
scoring worse than generated English is an ASR observation, not evidence that
the generated voice is more natural than a person.
